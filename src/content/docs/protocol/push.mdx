A relay keeps a list of devices by 1-byte tag. A sender asks it to wake every device under a tag; each woken device checks storage for itself. The relay never sees content, sender or account.

## Registering a device

<Steps>

1. The client reads the relay's public key from the push stream's metadata and checks it against the relay's known address.
2. It seals `{tag, subscription}` to that key (ECDH, HKDF-SHA256, AES-256-GCM) and publishes it on the push stream under a throwaway key.
3. The relay opens it and files the endpoint under the tag: a Web Push subscription or an FCM token.

</Steps>

**Tag:** the first byte of `keccak256("channel:" + streamId)`, or `"native:"` for gated channels. With 256 tags, each is shared by many channels.

## Waking devices

<Steps>

1. The sender publishes `{tag, pow, nonce, epoch, channelType, timestamp}` under a fresh key. The proof-of-work needs 4 leading zero hex digits over `tag:epoch:nonce`, with a 10-second epoch.
2. The relay checks the proof, accepting one epoch either side, and wakes every device under the tag: about 1 in 256 of all registrations.
3. Each device reads the newest message of its streams under that tag from their storage providers, and decides whether there is anything new.

</Steps>

`channelType` is `public` for Open, Protected and direct messages, `private` for gated channels, and `keys` for key requests.

## The relay

It stores tag, endpoint or FCM token, and timestamps, and counts events. Registrations do not expire by age; the proof-of-work is the only rate limit. It sends Web Push with VAPID, and data-only FCM messages when configured. The public cannot subscribe to the push stream.

<Aside title="How the official clients do it">
Reads of gated streams and inboxes are signed. Android signs them itself; the web service worker holds no key, so it asks an open Pombo window to sign and stays silent when none is open. A `keys` wake never shows a notification. What each notification shows is in [Notifications](/use/notifications/).
</Aside>
